# Rebuild EVA Cage Catwalk Layout — Minimum Supported Service-Deck Topology

## Scope

One narrow research unit: **human-scale catwalk / service-deck topology visible around a Rebuild-era EVA cage**, with emphasis on what can be supported by official or officially supervised reconstructions.

This cycle does **not** attempt to settle Entry Plug access geometry, exact cage wall dimensions, crane layout, pipe routing, lower pit/liquid, or the launch-platform restraint system.

## Continuity

Primary target: **Rebuild (`:序`-anchored visual language)**.

Evidence is drawn mainly from officially promoted Rebuild-era reconstructions and licensed exhibit imagery:

- `EVANGELION:WORLD — 実物大初号機建造計画` / Seventh Cage reconstruction (2010), made with Studio Khara cooperation.
- SMALL WORLDS Evangelion Hangar (2020–), presented by EVA-INFO and SMALL WORLDS as a moving 1/80 Evangelion hangar / launch reconstruction.

These are **not treated as direct film-production setting sheets**. They are used as official-meta / visual-reference evidence only.

TV / EoE: **Unknown in this cycle**. No cross-continuity merge.

## Summary

The strongest supported catwalk interpretation is **sparse, functional human access attached to the cage architecture**, not a dense stack of continuous rings surrounding the EVA.

Current evidence supports:

1. a broad **floor-level maintenance apron** at EVA foot level;
2. at least one **raised wall-mounted service balcony** with guardrails and stair access in the officially promoted SMALL WORLDS hangar environment;
3. lateral **guarded access / stair circulation beside the EVA** in the Khara-cooperated full-scale Seventh Cage reconstruction.

Current evidence does **not** support a fixed count such as `5–8 catwalk levels`. That number remains an unaudited Foundation claim and should not be built into the scene hierarchy as canon.

For Web 3D, the safest prototype is therefore a modular system of **one main floor apron + a small number of side-wall service decks/stairs**, with vertical positions adjustable until production-sheet or frame-level evidence resolves exact levels.

## Findings

### Finding 1 — A human-scale maintenance apron exists directly in front of / beneath the EVA bays

**Claim:**
The cage blockout should include a broad human-operable floor/apron at EVA foot level rather than placing the EVA in an inaccessible vertical void.

**Evidence class:** `VISUAL_OBSERVATION` + `OFFICIAL_META`

**Confidence:** High for the existence of a human-scale floor zone; Medium for exact width/depth.

**Evidence:**
SMALL WORLDS' official Evangelion Hangar imagery shows Units 02, 00, and 01 in adjacent bays with numerous human-scale staff figures distributed across a continuous floor area in front of the machines. Hazard-striped foot/base zones separate the EVA bay face from the staff floor.

EVA-INFO and SMALL WORLDS both describe the hangar as an active maintenance/launch environment where NERV staff work and the EVAs are prepared for launch.

This is sufficient to support a **floor-level circulation and maintenance band** as a first-order spatial element.

**Sources:**
- https://smallworlds.jp/area/eva_hangar/
- Official SMALL WORLDS hangar image: https://smallworlds.jp/wp/wp-content/themes/smallworlds/area/eva_hangar/03.jpg
- https://www.eva-info.jp/9321
- https://www.eva-info.jp/20044

**Web 3D classification:** `Must be geometry`

### Finding 2 — The official hangar environment contains a raised wall-mounted service balcony with a stair connection

**Claim:**
At least one **raised human-scale service deck** with handrails and stair access is a defensible cage-environment element.

**Evidence class:** `VISUAL_OBSERVATION`

**Confidence:** High that this element exists in the official SMALL WORLDS hangar representation; Low–Medium for direct film-continuity equivalence.

**Evidence:**
SMALL WORLDS' own hangar-area image shows NERV-scale personnel standing on a narrow wall-mounted balcony with an outer guardrail. A stair flight descends from the platform to a lower level. Structural bracing is visible below the deck.

The image confirms a topology of:

`wall / service opening → raised balcony → stair → lower circulation level`

It does **not** show the EVA in the same frame, so the balcony's exact vertical relationship to head, chest, waist, or Entry Plug height cannot be established from this image alone.

**Sources:**
- https://smallworlds.jp/area/eva_hangar/
- Official SMALL WORLDS image: https://smallworlds.jp/wp/wp-content/themes/smallworlds/area/eva_hangar/04.jpg

**Web 3D classification:** `Must be geometry` + `Can be instanced` for repeated rail/stair modules.

### Finding 3 — Lateral guarded access beside Unit-01 is consistent with the Khara-cooperated Seventh Cage reconstruction

**Claim:**
A Rebuild-inspired cage should reserve **lateral circulation space beside the EVA**, rather than surrounding it only with frontal floor access.

**Evidence class:** `OFFICIAL_META` + `VISUAL_OBSERVATION` + `SECONDARY`

**Confidence:** Medium.

**Evidence:**
Fuji-Q's official 2010 press material states that the full-scale Unit-01 bust reconstructs the **Seventh Cage** and that the pavilion was produced with Studio Khara's full cooperation. The official press image shows guarded side access elements flanking the Unit-01 reconstruction.

A contemporaneous GIGAZINE photo report of the same reconstruction explicitly describes a view from the **stairs beside Unit-01** (`初号機わきの階段`).

Because the attraction also had to accommodate visitors, these stairs/rails cannot be assumed to reproduce film geometry one-to-one. Their value is narrower: they reinforce the design logic that **human circulation occurs laterally along cage walls / beside the EVA**.

**Sources:**
- https://www.fujikyu.co.jp/data/news_pdf/20100728103552_HLevangelion20100726.pdf
- https://www.evangelion.jp/2_0/blog/archive272.html
- https://gigazine.net/news/20100723_fujiq_eva_evangelion_1st/

**Web 3D classification:** `Must be geometry`

### Finding 4 — Current evidence does not support a fixed 5–8 catwalk-level count

**Claim:**
The project should **not** encode `5–8 catwalk levels` as canon or as a fixed modeling requirement.

**Evidence class:** `UNKNOWN`

**Confidence:** High that the count is currently unverified.

**Evidence:**
The Gemini Foundation proposes multiple (`5–8`) catwalk levels, but this cycle found no official source, production sheet, or official reconstruction description that defines that count.

The official SMALL WORLDS frontal hangar image emphasizes a large floor apron and bay-wall machinery; it does not visibly present five to eight continuous stacked catwalks around the three EVA bays. A separate official image proves at least one raised service balcony and a stair, but not a specific total level count.

Absence in an exhibition image is not proof that the film cage lacks additional decks. The correct result is therefore `UNKNOWN`, not a contradictory fixed number.

**Sources:**
- Repository: `docs/research/raw-reports/gemini/0001-foundation-visual.md` (unaudited research material)
- https://smallworlds.jp/area/eva_hangar/

**Web 3D classification:** `Unknown`

### Finding 5 — Sparse side access is safer than continuous circumferential catwalk rings for the first 3D blockout

**Claim:**
For the first Web 3D prototype, catwalks should be implemented as **modular side-wall decks and stairs**, not as several complete rings encircling each EVA.

**Evidence class:** `INFERENCE`

**Confidence:** Medium–High as a production recommendation; not a canon claim.

**Evidence:**
All supported visual references in this cycle are compatible with **localized access**:

- a large staff apron at the feet;
- a wall-mounted service balcony;
- stair/guarded lateral access beside Unit-01.

None requires a full 360-degree walkway around the EVA or a repeated stack of continuous rings.

**3D consequence:**
Localized modules preserve the heavy negative-space character of the cage, maintain clear restraint/launch swept volumes, and make later repositioning easier when Entry Plug and maintenance-access research supplies stronger vertical anchors.

**Sources:**
- https://smallworlds.jp/area/eva_hangar/
- https://www.fujikyu.co.jp/data/news_pdf/20100728103552_HLevangelion20100726.pdf

## Visual Observations

### SMALL WORLDS — three-bay frontal view

Observed from the official hangar image:

- Three EVA bays are laterally repeated.
- Human figures occupy a broad floor/apron in front of the bays.
- The main bay face is dominated by large wall panels / restraint architecture rather than dense stacked walkways.
- Hazard markings at the EVA feet create a legible boundary between machine bay and staff floor.
- Human figures are visually tiny relative to the EVA, making the floor apron a strong scale cue.

Not established:

- whether hidden catwalks exist behind or between bay walls;
- the vertical level of any upper maintenance decks;
- exact widths, railing height, or floor grid dimensions.

### SMALL WORLDS — service balcony image

Observed:

- narrow rectangular balcony projecting from a wall;
- outer guardrail;
- several human figures on deck;
- stair descending from the deck;
- diagonal / framed support structure below;
- large dark negative space beyond the balcony.

This is useful as a **human-scale module reference** but not as an exact placement reference relative to the EVA body.

### Fuji-Q Seventh Cage reconstruction

Observed / reported:

- side guardrail/access elements adjacent to the Unit-01 bust;
- stairs beside Unit-01 reported by contemporary photo coverage;
- dark room with strongly localized illumination.

Visitor-safety adaptation is a major confounder; exact dimensions and film fidelity are therefore not assumed.

## Spatial Implications

A conservative cage circulation blockout should reserve:

1. **Floor apron** — continuous human circulation in front of the EVA bay face.
2. **Side-wall service strip** — space for localized balcony modules, wall openings, and stairs.
3. **Vertical stair envelope** — enough free volume for stairs to connect raised service decks to lower floors without crossing restraint / launch swept volumes.
4. **EVA clearance void** — large uninterrupted vertical negative space around most of the body.

Do not use dense multi-level rings to fill the void unless later evidence demands them.

## Human-Scale Implications

The catwalk system is one of the strongest scale cues available to the player.

Prioritize:

- visible human silhouettes on deck;
- standard-looking guardrails;
- stair flights with recognizable tread cadence;
- doors / service openings at deck endpoints;
- deck thickness visibly much smaller than EVA limb dimensions.

Exact real-world dimensions such as a 1.1 m rail height may be used later as **production reference**, but are not canon evidence from this cycle.

## 3D Modeling Implications

### Must be geometry

- Main floor/apron slab.
- Raised service balcony deck.
- Primary stairs.
- Outer guardrails when close enough to affect silhouette.
- Large wall openings / supports that anchor deck endpoints.

### Can be instanced

- Rail posts.
- Repeated stair stringer / support modules.
- Deck brackets.
- Light fixtures attached to catwalk modules.

### Can be procedural

- Grating panel repetition.
- Repeated railing spans between unique junctions.
- Stair tread repetition once rise/run is chosen as a production parameter.

### Can be texture / material

- Fine grating perforation at medium distance.
- anti-slip surface detail;
- paint wear / dirt.

### Can be decal

- deck IDs;
- warning lines;
- load / access markings;
- arrows and no-entry zones.

### Can be simplified at distance

- railing infill;
- stair tread geometry;
- small brackets and fasteners.

### Requires unique modeling

- any deck that interfaces directly with an Entry Plug, restraint assembly, or unique cage-wall opening.

## Implementation Recommendation

Use an adjustable hierarchy rather than hard-coded levels:

```text
cage_circulation
├─ floor_apron
├─ service_deck_A
│  ├─ deck
│  ├─ railing
│  └─ stair_A
├─ service_deck_B_optional
│  ├─ deck
│  ├─ railing
│  └─ stair_B
└─ entry_plug_access_reserved
```

`service_deck_B_optional` should remain disabled / provisional until stronger evidence appears.

Vertical positions should be parameters tied to EVA-relative landmarks rather than arbitrary world-space numbers.

## Lighting / Material Implications

The official reconstructions emphasize a useful contrast:

- catwalks remain readable through edge highlights / localized work light;
- surrounding vertical void remains substantially darker;
- staff silhouettes on deck become scale references against the dark background.

For Web 3D, do not flood-light every walkway. A small number of localized fixtures or emissive strips can make the deck readable while preserving cage depth.

This is partly `VISUAL_OBSERVATION` and partly a production recommendation; no canonical photometric values are established.

## Scale Evidence

- **Official:** SMALL WORLDS presents the Evangelion environment at 1/80 miniature scale, but the exhibition's exact dimensions are not adopted as film dimensions.
- **Relative:** floor-level humans are tiny compared with the EVA leg/foot region.
- **Relative:** raised service balcony width is human-scale and much smaller than EVA structural elements.
- **Unknown:** exact balcony elevation relative to EVA anatomy.
- **Unknown:** exact number of raised catwalk levels.
- **Unknown:** exact stair rise/run and railing dimensions.

No millimeter-level values should be extracted from exhibit photographs.

## Unknowns

- Exact number of catwalk levels in `:序` film continuity.
- Which EVA-relative heights have permanent decks: ankle, knee, waist, chest, shoulder, head, Entry Plug.
- Whether each bay has identical deck topology or shared cross-bay walkways.
- Whether decks cross in front of the EVA or remain lateral / rearward.
- Whether catwalks retract before launch.
- Exact relationship between catwalks and restraint-panel swept volume.
- Exact relationship between catwalks and Entry Plug insertion / pilot boarding.
- Whether the Fuji-Q side stair reflects film architecture or visitor-circulation adaptation.
