# Rebuild EVA Cage Human-Scale Circulation — Floor Apron, Raised Wall Access, and Stair Hierarchy

## Scope

One narrow research unit: the **human-scale circulation hierarchy inside the Rebuild-era EVA Cage**, limited to routes and scale cues that are defensible from official or officially promoted cage reconstructions.

This cycle focuses on:

- floor-level staff circulation near the EVA bays;
- raised wall-side service access;
- stairs and guardrails connecting human-scale levels;
- how staff placement contributes to perceived EVA scale.

This cycle does **not** attempt to settle Entry Plug access, exact catwalk level count, crane access, doors/hatches, Cage-to-launch-shaft networks, or NERV-wide circulation.

## Continuity

Primary target: **Rebuild**, especially the visual language of `ヱヴァンゲリヲン新劇場版` as represented by officially promoted / supervised reconstructions.

Primary evidence:

- SMALL WORLDS Evangelion Hangar, explicitly described in its English official page as the **Cage from Rebuild of Evangelion**.
- SMALL WORLDS official hangar images `03.jpg` and `04.jpg`.
- EVA-INFO official promotion of the SMALL WORLDS hangar.
- Fuji-Q `EVANGELION:WORLD` Seventh Cage reconstruction as contextual official-meta evidence only.

TV / EoE circulation geometry: **Unknown in this cycle**. No cross-continuity merge.

## Summary

The strongest currently defensible human-circulation model is a **two-tier minimum hierarchy**:

1. a **broad floor-level maintenance / circulation apron** running in front of the EVA bays and occupied by many human-scale NERV workers;
2. at least one **raised wall-side service deck** with guardrails, connected vertically by a **guarded stair** to a lower level.

The official SMALL WORLDS English page explicitly frames the staff workers as a scale-comparison device: the Evangelions appear gigantic relative to the small workers, and visitors are encouraged to view the scene from a NERV staff member's eye level. This means human circulation elements are not merely decorative clutter: they are one of the strongest available tools for communicating EVA scale in a walkable Web 3D scene.

Current evidence does **not** support assigning real-world-standard dimensions to the railings, stairs, or walkway widths, nor does it establish a dense multi-level circulation network. A safe prototype is therefore a **large floor apron + sparse wall-side deck/stair modules**, with exact heights and counts remaining adjustable.

## Findings

### Finding 1 — Floor level is a primary human-operable circulation zone

**Claim:**
The cage blockout should include a broad human-operable floor apron at the feet/front of the EVA bays, rather than treating the lower cage as inaccessible void.

**Evidence class:** `VISUAL_OBSERVATION` + `OFFICIAL_META`

**Confidence:** High for the official exhibit representation; Medium for exact film geometry.

**Evidence:**
The official SMALL WORLDS hangar image `03.jpg` shows Units 02, 00, and 01 aligned in adjacent bays with a broad, visually continuous floor plane directly in front of their feet. Numerous miniature staff figures occupy this plane across the bay frontage. SMALL WORLDS' official page states that the EVAs are stored and maintained in the cages and specifically highlights NERV staff in the hangar.

The image is exhibition geometry, not a production setting sheet, so it supports the **existence and function of a human-scale apron**, not its exact depth, markings, or structural thickness in film continuity.

### Finding 2 — Raised personnel access can be wall-side rather than wrapping the EVA

**Claim:**
A defensible cage circulation prototype can place raised personnel access primarily **along the wall / cage side**, instead of surrounding the EVA with arbitrary full-width or full-perimeter catwalk rings.

**Evidence class:** `VISUAL_OBSERVATION`

**Confidence:** High for SMALL WORLDS exhibit geometry; Low-Medium for exact Rebuild production geometry.

**Evidence:**
The official SMALL WORLDS image `04.jpg` shows a raised service platform projecting from / running along a cage wall. The platform has a guardrail and carries human figures. The visible geometry reads as a **localized wall-side balcony/service deck**, not a bridge spanning directly across the EVA bay.

This supports sparse lateral access as a conservative spatial pattern. It does not prove that all Rebuild cage decks are wall-mounted or that no EVA-crossing access bridge exists elsewhere.

**Sources:**

- https://smallworlds.jp/wp/wp-content/themes/smallworlds/area/eva_hangar/04.jpg

**Web 3D classification:** `Must be geometry` for the principal deck silhouette; repeated modules `Can be instanced`.

### Finding 3 — A stair provides explicit vertical continuity between personnel levels

**Claim:**
The human circulation system should include at least one explicit stair connection between a raised service deck and a lower circulation level.

**Evidence class:** `VISUAL_OBSERVATION`

**Confidence:** High for official exhibit geometry; Low-Medium for exact film placement.

**Evidence:**
SMALL WORLDS official image `04.jpg` clearly shows a stair descending beneath the raised platform, with guardrails on the stair and platform edges. This is stronger evidence for vertical personnel circulation than simply assuming ladders or lifts from generic industrial design practice.

No exact stair count, rise, width, angle, or destination can be established from the available official image.

**Sources:**

**Web 3D classification:** stair flights `Must be geometry`; repeating treads/rail posts may `Can be instanced` or procedurally generated.

### Finding 4 — Guardrails are a high-value scale cue, not only a safety detail

**Claim:**
Guardrails should be treated as visually important human-scale geometry because they establish the size of decks and stairs relative to the EVA.

**Evidence class:** `VISUAL_OBSERVATION` + `INFERENCE`

**Confidence:** Medium-High for visual function; exact dimensions Unknown.

**Evidence:**
The raised SMALL WORLDS service platform and stair use clearly legible railings. When a human figure and a railing occupy the same deck, the viewer gains two familiar scale references before comparing them to the cage wall and EVA body.

The official SMALL WORLDS English page independently emphasizes that the Evangelions' gigantic scale becomes apparent relative to the small staff workers and encourages viewing from staff eye level. This supports deliberate retention of human-readable reference geometry in Web 3D.

### Finding 5 — NERV workers are an intentional scale-comparison layer in the official hangar presentation

**Claim:**
A walkable cage scene should preserve human-scale reference objects and/or personnel on both the floor apron and selected raised access points; removing all humans risks making the EVA read like a small model.

**Evidence class:** `OFFICIAL_META`

**Confidence:** High for SMALL WORLDS presentation intent; Medium as a general Web 3D translation principle.

**Evidence:**
The SMALL WORLDS English hangar page says visitors can appreciate the Evangelions' gigantic size compared with the small staff workers. The Japanese page likewise recommends photographing the EVA from a NERV staff member's eye level. In 2026, EVA-INFO and SMALL WORLDS again promoted a program placing 1/80-scale NERV-member figures specifically in the Evangelion hangar area, confirming that personnel remain an intentional visual layer in the official exhibit.

This does not establish where specific canonical workers stand in a film shot. It establishes that **human figures are an officially used scale language for the cage**.

## Visual Observations

From official SMALL WORLDS imagery:

- the floor apron is much broader than a narrow catwalk and supports multiple staff figures simultaneously;
- the raised access deck is localized against the wall rather than visibly wrapping the entire EVA;
- the raised deck has a simple, legible railing silhouette;
- a stair descends from the raised deck to a lower level, creating an explicit vertical route;
- staff figures occur at more than one elevation, which gives the viewer repeated human-scale anchors;
- the dark background makes pale deck/rail geometry and human figures useful depth markers against the large cage volume.

These are observations of an official exhibit representation, not canonical dimensional measurements.

## Spatial Implications

A conservative circulation graph for the initial cage prototype is:

```text
[BAY-FRONT FLOOR APRON]
          |
          | stair / vertical transition
          v
[WALL-SIDE RAISED SERVICE DECK]
```

This should be interpreted as a **minimum supported topology**, not the complete canonical route network.

Recommended scene hierarchy:

- `cage/circulation/floor-apron`
- `cage/circulation/side-deck-*`
- `cage/circulation/stair-*`
- `cage/circulation/rail-*`
- `cage/circulation/staff-reference-*`

Do not hard-code an arbitrary number of deck levels. Additional modules should remain optional until stronger evidence is found.

## Human-Scale Implications

- Player spawn / first-person viewpoints near floor-apron level can reproduce the official exhibit's staff-eye-level scale effect.
- Raised decks provide a second human-scale band against the EVA torso/wall, strengthening vertical scale perception without inventing numerous levels.
- Railings, stairs, doors later added to these routes should remain dimensionally coherent with a human avatar, but **real-world standard values must be labeled production recommendations, not canon**.
- Multiple staff silhouettes distributed across floor and deck levels can provide scale without requiring close-up character detail.

## 3D Modeling Implications

### Must be geometry

- broad floor apron / service floor silhouette;
- principal raised service deck;
- stair flight and landings that affect player navigation;
- guardrails within player interaction distance.

### Can be instanced / procedural

- repeated railing posts and rail segments;
- stair treads when generated from a modular stair system;
- repeated worker figures in medium/far distance;
- small supports beneath service platforms.

### Can be decal / texture

- floor hazard striping;
- maintenance-zone labels;
- minor deck-edge markings.

### Navigation implementation

Build separate walkable navigation surfaces for the floor apron and raised deck, linked only through the verified/provisional stair route. This prevents the Web 3D implementation from implying nonexistent free movement through EVA restraint hardware or walls.

If the launch animation later crosses the floor apron, worker/path states should be dynamically gated rather than assuming personnel can remain in the rail-transfer envelope during launch operations. This is a **production recommendation / inference**, not canon evidence.

## Lighting / Material Implications

The official images support preserving the circulation silhouette against a dark cage background:

- brighter deck/rail edge readability is more important than uniformly lighting the entire wall;
- human figures and railings should not disappear into pure black at normal player viewing distance;
- no specific light intensity, color temperature, metal roughness, or PBR value is established by this research unit.

## Scale Evidence

- **Relative / Official-meta:** SMALL WORLDS explicitly presents EVA size in comparison with miniature NERV staff.
- **Relative / Visual observation:** staff figures occupy the floor apron and raised deck; guardrails and stairs provide familiar human-scale references.
- **Unknown:** exact apron width, deck width, guardrail height, stair dimensions, deck elevation, and distance from EVA.
- **Do not use as cage scale:** the `100 meters or more` value on SMALL WORLDS / EVA-INFO refers to the injection site / launch facility description, not securely to the cage chamber or circulation decks.
